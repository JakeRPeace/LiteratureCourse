# Literature Course Core Rules

This document serves as the single source of truth for all AI agents (Cursor, Google Antigravity, etc.) working on the LiteratureCourse project.

## 📚 Study Guide Documentation
When creating or enhancing book documentation, ALWAYS follow the three-part system established in Course 101.

### 📝 Templates
Use the global templates located in:
`00-09 Templates and Resources/01 Study Guide Templates/`

1. **01 Pre-Reading Guide.md**: Essential context, literary significance, and historical framework.
2. **02 Chapter-by-Chapter Notes.md**: Granular, section-by-section breakdown with plot, themes, and questions.
3. **03 Post-Reading Analysis.md**: Deep-dive thematic, symbolic, and critical analysis.

### 🎓 Quality Standards
- **Level:** All documentation must be at a **graduate/scholarly level**.
- **Depth:** Avoid surface-level summaries. Dive deep into literary techniques, historical nuances, and critical interpretations (e.g., Post-Colonial, Feminist, Psychoanalytic).
- **Consistency:** Use the structure defined in `00-09 Templates and Resources/01 Study Guide Templates/STUDY GUIDE STRUCTURE.md`.

## 📂 Organization & Management
- Follow the **Johnny.Decimal** system as structured in the project folders.
- Avoid loose files in book directories.
- Ensure all courses follow the chronological order of their respective syllabuses.
- **Always update READMEs:** When modifying a course's reading list, syllabus, or structure, ALWAYS ensure the corresponding course `README.md` and the root `README.md` are updated to reflect the changes.

## 🚫 Anti-Duplication Rule
- **Check before adding:** Before adding a book to a new course, check `00-09 Templates and Resources/00 System Management/00.00 Master Index.md` or search the repository to ensure the book isn't already included in another course.
- **Exceptions:** Books should only be duplicated if there is a strong thematic reason to study them in multiple contexts (e.g., *Fahrenheit 451* could be in Sci-Fi and Political). However, the preference is to keep the collection diverse and unique.

## 📝 Documentation Guidelines
- **Consistency:** Maintain a consistent tone and style across all documentation.
- **Accuracy:** Verify all information (dates, authors, titles, etc.) is accurate and up-to-date.
- **Clarity:** Ensure documentation is clear and easy to understand, with proper formatting and organization.
- **Depth:** Avoid surface-level summaries. Dive deep into literary techniques, historical nuances, and critical interpretations (e.g., Post-Colonial, Feminist, Psychoanalytic).
- **Critical Analysis:** Include critical analysis and interpretation of the text, including themes, symbolism, and literary devices.
- **Scholarly Tone:** Maintain a scholarly tone and use proper academic language and terminology.
- **Documentation:** Document all sources used in the documentation, including books, articles, and other resources.
